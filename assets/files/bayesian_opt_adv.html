Step 1 – Marginal (evidence) for a candidate design

Integrate the parameter out:

p(y∣ξ)=∫p(y∣θ,ξ) p(θ) dθp(y\mid\xi)=\int p(y\mid\theta,\xi)\,p(\theta)\,d\thetap(y∣ξ)=∫p(y∣θ,ξ)p(θ)dθ

This is the predictive distribution of the not-yet-observed outcome. It’s the only place the prior enters, and it’s what makes EIG computable before running the experiment.

Step 2 – Posterior you would get, for a hypothetical yyy

p(θ∣y,ξ)=p(y∣θ,ξ) p(θ)p(y∣ξ)p(\theta\mid y,\xi)=\frac{p(y\mid\theta,\xi)\,p(\theta)}{p(y\mid\xi)}p(θ∣y,ξ)=p(y∣ξ)p(y∣θ,ξ)p(θ)​

Step 3 – Information gain for that one outcome

IG(ξ,y)=DKL ⁣(p(θ∣y,ξ) ∥ p(θ))=∫p(θ∣y,ξ)log⁡p(θ∣y,ξ)p(θ)dθ\mathrm{IG}(\xi,y)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(p(\theta\mid y,\xi)\,\|\,p(\theta)\big)=\int p(\theta\mid y,\xi)\log\frac{p(\theta\mid y,\xi)}{p(\theta)}d\thetaIG(ξ,y)=DKL​(p(θ∣y,ξ)∥p(θ))=∫p(θ∣y,ξ)logp(θ)p(θ∣y,ξ)​dθ

Careful point worth having in your back pocket: the “entropy reduction” form H[p(θ)]−H[p(θ∣y,ξ)]H[p(\theta)]-H[p(\theta\mid y,\xi)]H[p(θ)]−H[p(θ∣y,ξ)] is not equal to this KL for a single yyy – a surprising outcome can even increase posterior entropy. They coincide only after averaging over yyy (Step 4).

Step 4 – Average over outcomes you haven’t seen yet

  EIG(ξ)=Ep(y∣ξ)[DKL(p(θ∣y,ξ)∥p(θ))]  \;\mathrm{EIG}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}_{p(y\mid\xi)}\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p(\theta\mid y,\xi)\|p(\theta))\big]\;EIG(ξ)=Ep(y∣ξ)​[DKL​(p(θ∣y,ξ)∥p(θ))]

Substituting Bayes’ rule and swapping the order of integration gives the three equivalent forms you can quote on the slide:

So EIG is the mutual information between parameters and outcome under the design. The middle form is the useful intuition: pick the experiment whose outcome you are most uncertain about overall, minus the part of that uncertainty that is just measurement noise. Ambiguity is good; noise is not.

The design step is then ξ⋆=arg⁡max⁡ξEIG(ξ)\xi^\star=\arg\max_\xi \mathrm{EIG}(\xi)ξ⋆=argmaxξ​EIG(ξ).

Step 5 – Actually evaluating it

p(y∣ξ)p(y\mid\xi)p(y∣ξ) is an intractable integral, so this is a nested expectation. Standard estimator (nested Monte Carlo):

EIG^=1N∑n=1N[log⁡p(yn∣θn,ξ)−log⁡1M∑m=1Mp(yn∣θn,m,ξ)]\hat{\mathrm{EIG}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\log p(y_n\mid\theta_n,\xi)-\log\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}p(y_n\mid\theta_{n,m},\xi)\right]EIG^=N1​n=1∑N​[logp(yn​∣θn​,ξ)−logM1​m=1∑M​p(yn​∣θn,m​,ξ)]

with θn∼p(θ)\theta_n\sim p(\theta)θn​∼p(θ), yn∼p(y∣θn,ξ)y_n\sim p(y\mid\theta_n,\xi)yn​∼p(y∣θn​,ξ), and inner contrastive draws θn,m∼p(θ)\theta_{n,m}\sim p(\theta)θn,m​∼p(θ).

  • Bias is O(1/M)O(1/M)O(1/M) and positive (Jensen on the inner log) – it flatters your design. Optimal budget scaling is M∝TM\propto\sqrt{T}M∝T​, N∝TN\propto\sqrt{T}N∝T​ for total cost TTT.
  • Cheaper variants: PCE/InfoNCE-style bounds, Barber–Agakov variational lower bound, VNMC upper bound. Sandwiching with a lower + upper bound is the honest way to report it.

The formula here works like this,

  • For the first term, sample NNN of θ\thetaθ values and for each of the θ\thetaθ values, sample yny_nyn​ which is composed of the computed exact value from the surrogate or physics mocel and the noise (e.g., Poisson noise). For the sampled yny_nyn​, calculate its probability p(yn∣θn,ξ)p(y_n\mid\theta_n,\xi)p(yn​∣θn​,ξ).

  • For the second term, we stay with the sampled yny_nyn​ in the previous step and we sample MMM of θ\thetaθ values and we compute the probability to obtain yny_nyn​ for each of the sampled θ\thetaθ values, giving us p(yn∣θn,m,ξ)p(y_n\mid\theta_{n,m},\xi)p(yn​∣θn,m​,ξ).
